# Hormonal birth control and the cycle

*Worth understanding because it changes the whole picture.*

## Combined pill (oestrogen + progestin)

Suppresses ovulation. The bleed during the placebo/break week isn't a real period — it's **withdrawal bleeding** caused by stopping the hormones, and it's typically lighter and less painful. It exists largely for historical and reassurance reasons, not medical necessity; skipping the break to avoid bleeding is generally considered safe, but discuss it with your prescriber.

## Progestin-only pill (mini-pill)

May or may not suppress ovulation depending on type. Bleeding patterns are often unpredictable.

## Hormonal IUD (e.g. Mirena, Kyleena)

Thins the endometrium locally. Often dramatically reduces bleeding and cramps; many users' periods become very light or stop entirely, which is safe. Frequently prescribed specifically to treat heavy bleeding.

## Copper IUD

Non-hormonal, so ovulation continues normally — but it commonly makes periods **heavier and crampier**, especially in the first few months.

## Implant, injection, ring, patch

Each with its own bleeding profile; irregular spotting is common early on with the implant and injection.

## Beyond contraception

Hormonal methods are legitimately used to treat heavy bleeding, severe cramps, endometriosis, PCOS symptoms, PMDD, acne, and menstrual migraine.

## Coming off it

Cycles may take a few months to re-establish their own rhythm. Whatever your periods were like before starting, they'll typically return to roughly that — the contraception was managing symptoms, not curing the underlying cause.
