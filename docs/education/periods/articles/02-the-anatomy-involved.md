# The anatomy involved

*The organs — and the brain circuit — that make a cycle possible.*

## Ovaries

Two almond-sized organs holding all the eggs a person will ever have (roughly **1–2 million** at birth, declining to around **300,000–400,000** by puberty, of which only about **400** are ever ovulated). They also produce oestrogen and progesterone.

## Fallopian tubes

Two narrow tubes carrying the released egg toward the uterus. Fertilisation, when it happens, usually occurs here rather than in the uterus.

## Uterus (womb)

A muscular, pear-sized organ. Its inner lining, the **endometrium**, is what thickens and sheds each cycle. Its muscular wall, the **myometrium**, does the contracting that causes cramps.

## Cervix

The narrow neck at the base of the uterus opening into the vagina. Its mucus changes texture across the cycle, which is why cervical mucus is useful for tracking fertility.

## Vagina

The muscular canal through which menstrual fluid exits.

## Hypothalamus and pituitary gland

In the brain, not the pelvis — but they're the control room. They release the signalling hormones that tell the ovaries what to do, and they respond to feedback from the ovaries in turn. This brain–ovary loop is called the **HPO axis** (hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis), and it's why stress, illness, undereating, and overtraining can stop periods: the brain is part of the circuit.
