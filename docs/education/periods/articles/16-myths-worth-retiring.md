# Myths worth retiring

*Common claims, corrected.*

**"You can't get pregnant on your period."** You can. Sperm survive up to **5 days**, and people with short cycles can ovulate soon after bleeding ends.

**"A 28-day cycle is the correct one."** It's an average. Anything from **21 to 35 days** is normal.

**"Bad period pain is just part of being a woman."** Some discomfort is expected. Pain that stops you functioning is a symptom, not a rite of passage, and normalising it is precisely why conditions like endometriosis take years to diagnose.

**"You lose a lot of blood."** Typically **30–80 ml** total. It looks like more because it's mixed with tissue and mucus and spread over days.

**"Periods sync between people living together."** The best-designed studies don't support this. Cycles of different lengths inevitably drift in and out of alignment by chance, which is what people notice.

**"You shouldn't exercise, swim, or bathe on your period."** All fine, and exercise generally helps cramps. Tampons, cups, and discs all work for swimming.

**"PMS is imaginary or an excuse."** The physiology — hormone withdrawal acting on GABA and serotonin systems — is well documented. PMDD is a recognised clinical diagnosis.

**"Missing a period means you're pregnant."** It's the first thing to rule out, but stress, weight change, illness, exercise, thyroid problems, and PCOS are all common causes.

**"Skipping periods on the pill is dangerous."** The pill bleed isn't a real period and isn't a physiological requirement. Worth confirming with your prescriber, but it's generally considered safe.

**"Menopause happens overnight at 50."** Perimenopause is a transition that commonly spans several years.
