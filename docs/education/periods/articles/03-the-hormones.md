# The hormones that run the cycle

*Rise and fall matter more than absolute levels — and four hormones do most of the work.*

## GnRH (gonadotropin-releasing hormone)

Released in pulses by the hypothalamus. Sets the tempo for everything downstream.

## FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone)

From the pituitary. Recruits a cohort of follicles (fluid-filled sacs, each holding an immature egg) in the ovaries and stimulates them to mature.

## LH (luteinizing hormone)

Also from the pituitary. Rises gradually, then spikes sharply about **24–36 hours** before ovulation. That **LH surge** is the trigger that releases the egg, and it's what home ovulation predictor kits detect in urine.

## Oestrogen (mainly oestradiol)

Produced by the growing follicle. Rebuilds and thickens the endometrium, thins cervical mucus so sperm can travel, and peaks just before ovulation. Many people report their best energy, mood, and sleep during the high-oestrogen stretch.

## Progesterone

Produced after ovulation by the **corpus luteum**, the structure the emptied follicle becomes. It stabilises the endometrium, thickens cervical mucus again, and raises basal body temperature slightly. It also has a sedating, sometimes low-mood effect for many people. Its steep drop at the end of the cycle is what actually triggers the period.

## Testosterone

Present in smaller amounts, peaking around ovulation. Contributes to libido.

---

A useful mental model: **oestrogen builds, progesterone holds, and the withdrawal of progesterone releases.**
