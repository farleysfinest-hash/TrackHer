# PMS and PMDD

*Common luteal-phase symptoms — and a severe mood disorder that needs care, not endurance.*

## PMS (premenstrual syndrome)

Symptoms appearing in the luteal phase and resolving within a few days of bleeding starting. Around **3 in 4** menstruating people experience some version of it.

**Physical:** bloating, breast tenderness, fluid retention, headaches, acne, joint or muscle aches, food cravings, fatigue, disturbed sleep.

**Emotional/cognitive:** irritability, low mood, anxiety, tearfulness, reduced concentration, feeling overwhelmed, social withdrawal.

The mechanism isn't abnormal hormone levels — most people with PMS have entirely normal hormone profiles. It's **sensitivity to the change**, specifically to progesterone's breakdown product allopregnanolone and its effect on GABA receptors in the brain, plus serotonin fluctuations.

Helpful: consistent sleep, regular exercise, reduced alcohol and caffeine, adequate calcium and vitamin D, stress management. Hormonal contraception helps some people and worsens it for others.

## PMDD (premenstrual dysphoric disorder)

A severe, distinct condition recognised in the DSM-5, affecting roughly **2–5%** of menstruating people by strict prospectively-confirmed criteria (studies using looser retrospective criteria report up to **8%**). The defining feature is severity of *mood* symptoms: marked depression, hopelessness, intense irritability or anger, anxiety or tension, and mood swings, severe enough to disrupt work, study, or relationships.

Diagnosis requires **prospective symptom tracking across at least two cycles** — showing symptoms confined to the luteal phase and clearing after bleeding starts. This distinguishes PMDD from depression or anxiety that a period merely worsens.

It's treatable. Options include SSRIs (which for PMDD often work within days and can be taken luteal-phase-only rather than continuously), certain hormonal contraceptives, and in severe refractory cases GnRH agonists. **PMDD is not something to endure quietly** — it carries a genuinely elevated risk of self-harm and suicidal ideation, and getting help matters.

If your premenstrual mood symptoms are severe, keeping a simple daily rating log for two cycles before seeing a doctor will substantially speed up getting the right diagnosis.
