# What menstrual blood actually is

*It isn't purely blood — and that explains a lot of what people find alarming.*

Menstrual fluid is roughly half blood, plus endometrial tissue, cervical mucus, and vaginal secretions.

- **Bright red** — fresh, fast flow, typical of the heaviest days
- **Dark red, brown, or nearly black** — older blood that oxidised on its way out; very common at the start and end of a period
- **Pink or watery** — light flow diluted with cervical fluid
- **Small clots** — normal, especially on heavy days. The body releases anticoagulants to keep flow liquid, but on a heavy day it can't keep up. **Clots consistently larger than a 10p coin / a quarter (~2.5 cm) are worth mentioning to a doctor.**
- **Grey or foul-smelling discharge** — not normal; see a doctor, as it can indicate infection

Total loss averages **30–80 ml** across the whole period. Over **80 ml** is clinically classed as heavy (menorrhagia) — but nobody measures this at home, so practical markers are used instead. See [When to see a doctor](15-when-to-see-a-doctor.md).
