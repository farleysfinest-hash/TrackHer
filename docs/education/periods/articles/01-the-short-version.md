# The short version

*What a period is, and how it sits inside the wider cycle.*

A period is the shedding of the lining of the uterus. It happens when an egg released that month isn't fertilised, hormone levels drop, and the lining — which had been thickening in preparation for a pregnancy — breaks down and leaves the body through the vagina.

The period is just one visible part of a larger, roughly monthly hormonal cycle. That cycle typically runs **21 to 35 days** in adults, with bleeding lasting **2 to 7 days**. Most people lose only **30–80 ml** of blood in total — a few tablespoons — though it usually looks like far more.

Cycles begin at puberty (**menarche**, usually ages 10–15) and end at **menopause** (average around 51). In between, they can be knocked off course by stress, illness, weight change, exercise, travel, contraception, pregnancy, and breastfeeding.
