# The cycle, phase by phase

*Day 1 is the first day of proper bleeding — not spotting the day before.*

## Menstrual phase (days 1–5, roughly)

Progesterone and oestrogen are at their lowest. The endometrium loses its blood supply, breaks down, and is expelled by uterine contractions. Flow is usually heaviest on days 1–2 and tapers after.

Low hormones mean many people feel tired and low-energy in the first day or two, then noticeably better as oestrogen begins climbing again around days 3–4.

## Follicular phase (day 1 until ovulation)

Overlaps with the period, then continues after it. FSH matures a group of follicles; within about a week one becomes **dominant** and the rest degenerate. The dominant follicle pumps out rising oestrogen, which rebuilds the endometrium from a thin layer to several millimetres thick.

**This is the variable phase.** If someone's cycles are 24 days one month and 33 the next, it's almost always the follicular phase stretching or shortening. Stress, illness, and travel delay ovulation and therefore lengthen this phase.

## Ovulation (a single event, mid-cycle)

Rising oestrogen crosses a threshold and flips the pituitary from suppression into a burst — the LH surge. Roughly **24–36 hours** later the dominant follicle ruptures and releases its egg into the fallopian tube.

Signs, if you look for them:

- **Cervical mucus** becomes clear, slippery, and stretchy — commonly described as raw egg white
- **Mittelschmerz** — a one-sided pelvic twinge or ache lasting hours to a day, felt by around 1 in 5 people
- **Basal body temperature** rises about **0.3–0.5 °C** *after* ovulation and stays up (confirms it happened; doesn't predict it)
- Higher libido, and for some, slight spotting or breast tenderness

### Fertility window

The egg survives only **12–24 hours**. Sperm can survive up to **5 days** in fertile cervical mucus. So the fertile window is roughly the **5 days before ovulation plus the day of it — about 6 days total**, with the two days before ovulation being the most fertile.

## Luteal phase (ovulation to the next period)

The collapsed follicle becomes the corpus luteum and secretes progesterone, which makes the endometrium receptive to an embryo.

The luteal phase is remarkably **consistent at 12–14 days** in most people, regardless of total cycle length. This is the basis of a useful rule: if you know when your period started, you can estimate ovulation as roughly **14 days before the next period**, not 14 days after the last one.

If no embryo implants, the corpus luteum degenerates after about **10–12 days**. Progesterone and oestrogen fall sharply. The endometrium loses support, and the next period begins. If an embryo *does* implant, it produces **hCG** — the hormone pregnancy tests detect — which keeps the corpus luteum alive and progesterone high, so no period occurs.

Most PMS symptoms cluster in the last several days of this phase, as hormones fall.
