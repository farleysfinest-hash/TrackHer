# Endometriosis

Tissue similar to the uterine lining grows outside the uterus — on the ovaries, fallopian tubes, bowel, bladder, or pelvic wall. It responds to cycle hormones and bleeds, but has nowhere to go, causing inflammation, scarring, and adhesions.

Affects roughly **1 in 10** women and people with a uterus of reproductive age. Diagnosis has historically taken an average of **7–10 years** (studies range from about **1.5 to 11 years** depending on country), largely because severe period pain gets normalised — by patients and clinicians alike.

## Symptoms

Severe pelvic pain (often worse than "bad cramps" and often starting before the period), pain during or after sex, painful bowel movements or urination especially during periods, heavy bleeding, chronic fatigue, and difficulty conceiving. Notably, **pain severity doesn't correlate well with disease extent**.

## Diagnosis and treatment

Suspected clinically, sometimes visible on specialist ultrasound or MRI, definitively confirmed by laparoscopy. Treatment: pain management, hormonal suppression, and surgical excision. There's no cure, but it is manageable.

**If your period pain regularly stops you functioning, this is worth raising explicitly — and worth pushing on if you're brushed off.**
